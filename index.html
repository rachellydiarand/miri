Remember in the movie Contact when Ellie is on Vega with her dad and she starts asking him "The machine you built...." And he interjects "We didn't build it."

It's like most of the stuff here. There are lots of people around that did build a bunch of the stuff here, but let's face it. The majority of the quantity of stuff we see in our cities was not built by anyone alive. 100 year old houses. Sewer systems. Vegetation design. The location of paths. Water sources. Dams.

Just saying that we have all inherited a lot. Even someone who is 100 years old today was 24 years old when 90% of America had electricity.

Some ideas in the general public really bother me. This idea that a downsizing population is going to be overworked seems absurd. Sure they will be if we try to keep replacing everything we have for the baby boomer generation, but of course there is no logic in that. Those generations built stuff for those larger generations. And a lot of that is just sitting out there. Those larger generations will die off. They have to. And when they do, those buildings will be empty and available to other people.

If the younger generations are significantly smaller, there will be more of the big stuff for each one of them, without building. Sure, it will be degrading stuff. Old buildings. Old blenders. Old toasters. That's where the "fun" scrapper existence comes into play. The scavenger hunt for things that still work.

Trust me on this. It'll be fun to be alive and not have children. It's WAY BETTER than what would happen if you have children. If everyone continues replacement rate, it'll be a massive battle in the streets someday.

The job of the presidency as I see it is this figuring out what we can and can't do on an industrial scale. It comes down to the parts that factories need and how much of them we can make. It's a huge Tetris puzzle of engineering pieces and I'm stacked with ability to both make the judgment calls on what we need and what the dang objects are themselves. Completely stacked. I'm not some kind of personal judge. You all go on with your personal lives and all. I have my personal life too.

The only real things we'll be missing are mass transportation in a travel the world kind of way and the joy of the 50 hour work weeks at the construction site building the foundation. Like "things could be worse."
